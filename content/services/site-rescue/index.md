## When a site needs rescuing

Most rescues start with one of three situations.

**The platform is going away.** Your host announced it is shutting down, or the system your site runs on is so old that nobody supports it any more. Moving it isn't straightforward, and the clock is running.

**You're paying for more than you use.** A hosted editor and a monthly plan for a site that is really a handful of pages and a gallery. The fees keep arriving whether or not anything changes.

**The site is already gone.** The platform closed, the account lapsed, or the host disappeared, and nobody kept a copy. This is the hardest case, but it is not always a lost one.

## What I recover

Everything a visitor would have seen, and as much of what sat behind it as survives:

- every page and its text
- every image, at the best quality I can find, not the squashed copy that was on the page
- titles, captions, descriptions and the order things were in
- your menu and the structure of the site
- the look of the site, if you want it kept

Where an image only exists as a small thumbnail, I'll say so rather than quietly pass it off as the original.

## How it works

1. **You show me what you have.** Send the address, or whatever you remember of it. I look at what is still online, what you can export, and what has been kept elsewhere.
2. **I recover it.** From the live site, an export or a backup if there is one. If the site is gone, I dig through public web archives, which sometimes hold far more than they appear to. I'll tell you early what I expect to get back.
3. **I rebuild it.** Same design with the platform stripped out, or a fresh one built around your work. Every image is stored with the site itself, so nothing depends on a service that can disappear again.
4. **We check it together.** The new site is ready to look at before anything changes for your visitors. Your old site stays up until you're happy.
5. **It goes live, and it's yours.** On your domain, with your files in your hands. I show you how to update it yourself, or I'll do it for you.

## What you end up with

- **A site that loads fast.** Pages that took seconds now open almost instantly.
- **Little or nothing to pay to keep it running.** Plain sites like this are cheap or free to host, so the monthly platform fee goes away.
- **Nothing to log into, patch or renew** just to keep your site standing.
- **Your content, your domain, your files.** If you ever want someone else to take over, you hand them everything.
- **A site anyone can use**, on any device, with accessibility built in.

To a visitor, a rescue can look like nothing happened. Same site, same address, quicker. That is often the point.

## What a rescue can't do

I can only rebuild what I can find. Some things don't survive a platform closing: contact form submissions, comments, anything that lived in a database nobody backed up. If your site has an online shop or member logins, tell me up front. Those need a different conversation.

If the work was ever online, chances are parts of it can be recovered. I won't know how much until I look, and I'll tell you honestly what I find.
