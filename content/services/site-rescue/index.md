## When a Site Needs Rescuing

Most rescues start with one of three situations:

**The platform is going away.** Your hosting company announced it is shutting down, or the system your site runs on is so old that nobody supports it anymore.

**You're paying for more than you use.** A hosted editor and a monthly plan for what is really a handful of pages and a gallery.

**The site is already gone.** The platform closed, the account lapsed, or the host disappeared, and nobody kept a copy. This is the hardest case, but not always a lost one.

Sometimes the site is still up but it is falling apart: a "not secure" warning, broken images, vanished pages, a lapsed domain, a login nobody has, or pages that take seconds to appear. A small problem can often be fixed on its own. When the platform itself is the problem, a move is the lasting fix, and I'll tell you honestly which one I think you need.

## What I Recover

I recover what still exists, and I'll tell you early on how much that is:

- The pages and their text
- Your images, at the highest quality I can find
- Titles, captions, and descriptions in their proper order
- Your menu and the structure of the site
- The look of the site, if you want it kept, as closely as it can be reconstructed

Missing images can often be recovered. The original files may still be on the old host, in a backup, or in a web archive, even when the page showing them is broken. I will tell you if an image only exists as a small thumbnail.

## How It Works

1. **You show me what you have.** Send me the address, or whatever you remember of it. I look at what is still online, what you can export, and what has been kept elsewhere.
2. **I recover it.** From the live site, an export, or a backup, if there is one. If the site is gone, I dig through public web archives. I will tell you early on in the process what I expect to get back.
3. **I rebuild it.** The same design with the platform stripped out, or a fresh one built around your work. Images are stored within the site itself, so nothing depends on a service that can disappear.
4. **We check it together.** The new site is examined before going online.
5. **It goes live, and it's yours.** On your domain, with files that you own.

## Search Rankings, Links and Email

Moving a site shouldn't mean losing the visitors who already find it. I keep your existing addresses wherever that makes sense, redirect the ones that change, and carry over your page titles, descriptions and image text. If your email runs on your own domain, I leave its settings alone so it keeps working.

## What You End Up With

- **A fast site.** Plain pages usually open instantly.
- **Little or nothing to pay to keep it running.** Simple sites are cheap or free to host, so you are no longer paying a monthly platform fee. You will still pay a yearly domain renewal fee, because the domain name belongs to you.
- **Nothing to patch or log into to keep the public site standing.** Editing is optional, and I'll show you how to do it.
- **Your content, your domain, your files.** If you ever want someone else to take over, you hand them everything.

To a visitor, a rescue can look like the old site, only it will load quicker.

## What a Rescue Can't Do

I can only rebuild what I can find. Some things don't survive a platform closure: contact form submissions, comments, purchase orders, or anything in a database that wasn't backed up. Public web archives generally keep the pages as visitors saw them, not the original application or private data. Let me know if your site once had an online shop or member logins.
