## When a Site Needs Rescuing

Most rescues start with one of three situations.

**The platform is going away.** Your host announced it is shutting down, or the system your site runs on is so old that nobody supports it any more.

**You're paying for more than you use.** A hosted editor and a monthly plan for what is really a handful of pages and a gallery.

**The site is already gone.** The platform closed, the account lapsed, or the host disappeared, and nobody kept a copy. This is the hardest case, but not always a lost one.

Sometimes the site is still up but quietly falling apart: a "not secure" warning, broken images, vanished pages, a lapsed domain, a login nobody has, or pages that take seconds to appear. A small problem can often be fixed on its own. When the platform itself is the problem, a move is the lasting fix, and I'll tell you honestly which one I think you need.

## What I Recover

I recover what survives, and I'll tell you early how much that looks like:

- the pages and their text
- your images, at the best quality I can find rather than the squashed copy on the page
- titles, captions, descriptions and the order things were in
- your menu and the structure of the site
- the look of the site, if you want it kept, as closely as what remains allows

Missing images are often easier to get back than they look. The original files can still sit on the old host, in a backup, or in a web archive, even when the page that showed them is broken. Where an image only exists as a small thumbnail, I'll say so rather than pass it off as the original.

## How It Works

1. **You show me what you have.** Send the address, or whatever you remember of it. I look at what is still online, what you can export, and what has been kept elsewhere.
2. **I recover it.** From the live site, an export or a backup if there is one. If the site is gone, I dig through public web archives. I'll tell you early what I expect to get back.
3. **I rebuild it.** The same design with the platform stripped out, or a fresh one built around your work. Images are stored with the site itself, so nothing depends on a service that can disappear again.
4. **We check it together.** The new site is ready to look at before anything changes for your visitors.
5. **It goes live, and it's yours.** On your domain, with your files in your hands.

## Search Rankings, Links and Email

Moving a site shouldn't mean losing the people who already find it. I keep your existing addresses wherever that makes sense, redirect the ones that change, and carry over your page titles, descriptions and image text. If your email runs on your own domain, I leave its settings alone so it keeps working.

I can't promise your rankings or traffic will stay exactly as they were, and I won't pretend otherwise.

## What You End Up With

- **A fast site.** Plain pages like these usually open almost instantly.
- **Little or nothing to pay to keep it running.** Simple sites are cheap or free to host, so the monthly platform fee goes away. You still renew your domain name each year, since that's yours.
- **Nothing to patch or log into to keep the public site standing.** Editing is optional, and I'll show you how or do it for you.
- **Your content, your domain, your files.** If you ever want someone else to take over, you hand them everything.

To a visitor, a rescue can look much like before, only quicker.

## What a Rescue Can't Do

I can only rebuild what I can find. Some things don't survive a platform closing: contact form submissions, comments, orders, anything that lived in a database nobody backed up. Public web archives generally keep the pages as visitors saw them, not the original application or private data. If your site has an online shop or member logins, tell me up front. That needs a different conversation.
