Elisha Shapiro—the self-described LA Freak—is a Los Angeles conceptual artist whose cheerful nihilism has taken the form of the Nihilist Olympics, the National Nihilist Party, the Nihilism Expo, a television show, and an international film festival. His memoir, [*LA Freak*](https://link.amazon/A0iMl5PxF), looks back at the rebellious, alternative Los Angeles that shaped him. Since the late 1990s, **Nihilists’ Corner** has been the work’s home online: a hand-built maze of art, provocations, festival programs, political campaigns, newsletters, and wonderfully unreasonable images.

By 2026, the work was still alive but the infrastructure around it was failing. Elisha’s longtime hosting company had been absorbed into a larger provider, support had deteriorated, the visitor statistics had stopped working, and the server held years of duplicate folders and files he did not recognize. His own copies were out of date. With another annual hosting bill approaching, he needed someone to get the site somewhere safe.

The assignment was never to redesign it. The site’s raw, early-web appearance is part of the artwork. Making it conventionally modern would have erased the very thing worth preserving.

We copied and independently verified the entire hosting account: **1,291 files recovered**, including archives and working material that an ordinary website copier would have missed. From that collection we identified the live site—29 pages and 143 supporting images, PDFs, and other files—and preserved the rest in a complete private archive.

The public site moved to dependable, secure hosting with no annual hosting fee. We added working visitor statistics, version history, a proper missing-page response, and **36 permanent redirects** so bookmarks collected over decades still arrive where they should. Every public file and every redirect was checked individually, followed by a visual review that caught and repaired an image modern browsers had quietly stopped displaying.

Elisha still edits the pages in Dreamweaver, the tool he already knows. A simple publishing workflow now keeps every revision and puts his changes online in about a minute. One week after launch, he published his first update himself.

What he got was not a replacement for Nihilists’ Corner. He got **Nihilists’ Corner, still unmistakably itself**—with a complete archive, reliable statistics, dramatically lower costs, and the freedom to keep running it his way.
