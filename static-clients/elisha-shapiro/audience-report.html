Who’s actually reading your site

Your web server kept a record of every visit this year. Here is what it says about who comes to nihilists.net, what they read, and how they find you.

The Film Festival is what people come for

Setting aside the front page, the festival pages are read more than anything else on the site — and the old programs page is the single most-read page you have. Press coverage is close behind. People are looking up the festival’s history.

They come straight to you

Almost everyone who visits types the address or uses a bookmark. Search engines sent 141 visits in eight months — against more than 48,000 who arrived directly.

This says something worth knowing: your audience already knows you exist. They are not stumbling in from Google. That is why keeping your existing web addresses working mattered so much in the move — every old link and bookmark still lands where it should.

Why your old statistics looked like traffic was falling

The program on the old server that counted your visitors stopped running in June. It missed seventeen days in July entirely, and recorded nothing at all after 25 August.

So the dip you may have seen in the old stats pages is missing records, not missing readers. Corrected for the days it failed to count, your traffic is flat.

The new Cloudflare setup measures visits and page performance without depending on the old server’s scheduled statistics job. It is running on the staging site now and will receive a fresh production tag when nihilists.net launches.

One thing that sounds alarming and isn’t

Most of what reached your old server was not people. Automated programs constantly scan every website on the internet looking for weaknesses — they were asking your server for login pages and admin panels that your site has never had.

That is completely normal for a domain that has been online since 2002, and none of it got anywhere. The numbers on this page count only real people reading real pages; the automated noise has been stripped out. Your new host filters most of it before it ever arrives.
